\begin{abstract}
We report a paper-then-code reimplementation and extension study of
GCCP (Global-Consistent Comparative Pointwise) ranking, proposed by
Long et al.~\cite{long2025precise} at SIGIR 2025. GCCP augments
pointwise LLM ranking with an \emph{anchor} passage, constructed via
spectral multi-document summarization over the BM25 top-$m$ pool, and
aggregates the resulting contrastive score with a relevance-grading
score (RG-YN) using a post-aggregation framework called PAGC. We
re-implement the pipeline first from the paper text alone, then audit
it against the authors' released code to recover seven undocumented
hyperparameters that determine whether reproduction succeeds. We run
the full pipeline on TREC DL 2019/2020 and the eight paper-aligned
BEIR subsets across three Flan-T5 scales (Large/XL/UL2). The average
absolute NDCG@10 gap is $2$--$4\%$, with several per-dataset cells
matching or exceeding the original. We surface
seven implementation details that the paper does not document but
which silently determine whether reproduction succeeds, and we show
that one of them -- the case of the relevance-target tokens -- alone
collapses NDCG@10 from 0.66 to 0.24 on DL19. We further report that
(i) on the DL19 / Flan-T5-Large / BM25 cell that the paper's
Section~4.4.1 implicitly relies on, Borda / Condorcet / Copeland
outperform the paper's $\alpha{=}0.5$ linear aggregation by
$+1.1$~pts NDCG@10; across a wider 8-cell sweep, however, Borda is
on average tied with linear, so the original paper's reading
(``aggregation method does not matter much'') generalizes, (ii) replacing BM25 with an off-the-shelf E5
dense retriever lifts PAGC by $+1.5$ to $+4.2$~pts and exceeds the
paper's best published number on both DL19 and DL20 with Flan-T5-XL,
(iii) under a paired-bootstrap test (10{,}000 resamples) with
Holm-Bonferroni correction across 22 primary
(dataset $\times$ retrieval $\times$ model) settings, \textbf{GCCP alone
is Holm-significantly distinguishable from RG-YN in only 3/22 settings}
-- the largest BEIR set (DBPedia-Entity, $n_q{=}400$) and the
72B-scale decoder backbone (Qwen-2.5-72B-AWQ on DL19+DL20). PAGC vs
GCCP is Holm-significant in 5/22 with mixed signs -- 4 positive (all
BM25) and \textbf{1 negative} (DBPedia-Entity, $\Delta{=}{-}0.0144$,
$p_{\text{Holm}}{=}0.032$) -- so aggregating with RG-YN under a strong
dense retriever can hurt rather than help. PAGC vs RG-YN is
Holm-significant in 12/22 settings (all positive). The aggregation
step's value is not uniform across (model, retrieval) regimes, and
(iv) the spectral MDS anchor never wins on any of the 8 BEIR sets we
ran anchor ablations on -- Top-3 sentence composite or Random passage
wins each set, contradicting the paper's reported BEIR-aggregate
ordering; (v) the contrastive-anchor mechanism
transfers to large quantized decoder-only models -- Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct (4-bit AWQ on a single 48~GB GPU) reaches PAGC NDCG@10
$=0.7465$ on DL19, surpassing both our reproduced Flan-UL2 (0.7095)
and the paper's own reported Flan-UL2 (0.7321); and (vi) on the
full 8-set BEIR generalization under E5 + Flan-T5-XL, PAGC over
RG-YN holds (8/8 directional, 6/8 Holm-sig) but PAGC over
GCCP-alone is no longer reliable -- 0/8 Holm-sig positive, with
DBPedia-Entity actively flipping negative (see also finding iii).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ============================================================

Zero-shot LLM-based document ranking has converged on three
prompt families: \emph{listwise} (rank a list at once), \emph{pairwise}
(compare two documents), and \emph{pointwise} (score one document at a
time). The pointwise family is the cheapest -- $O(n)$ inferences for
$n$ candidates -- but the documents are scored independently, so the
LLM never sees how they relate to each other. Long et al.~\cite{long2025precise}
propose GCCP to bridge this gap: they insert an \emph{anchor} passage
-- a query-focused summary built from the top-$m$ candidates by spectral
multi-document summarization -- into a pointwise prompt and ask the
LLM to compare the candidate against the anchor. Aggregating the
resulting score with a standard relevance-grading score (PAGC)
yields, in their paper, listwise-competitive NDCG@10 at pointwise
cost.

The method is attractive because it is training-free, model-agnostic
in principle, and has no $O(n^2)$ explosion. We selected it for
reproduction because (i) it is recent, well-cited, and proposes a
clean architectural idea, (ii) the authors release code, allowing a
controlled audit of paper-vs.-implementation discrepancies, and (iii)
the method is small enough to be \emph{re-implementable} in a course
project, which is exactly the regime where reproducibility hazards
matter most.

This paper makes the following contributions:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,nosep]
    \item A paper-then-code reimplementation: we coded each of the
          four pipeline components from the paper text first, then
          consulted the authors' release only to resolve ambiguities
          (with each consultation logged in our project plan). The
          result reproduces the paper's headline numbers within
          $2$--$4\%$ NDCG@10 across three model scales and ten
          datasets, with several individual cells matching or
          exceeding the paper.

    \item A catalogue of seven undocumented implementation details
          discovered during reproduction (Section~\ref{sec:undocumented}),
          including a single-token case discrepancy that drops NDCG@10
          from 0.66 to 0.24.

    \item Paired-bootstrap significance tests (the original paper
          reports none) showing that GCCP alone is not statistically
          different from RG-YN, while PAGC vs.\ GCCP is highly
          significant -- the statistical signal lives in the
          aggregation step.

    \item Two extensions absent from the original paper: (i) E5 dense
          retrieval as the first stage, which on its own beats
          PAGC$+$BM25 on TREC DL; and (ii) decoder-only LLMs
          (LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct) adapted
          via chat templates.

    \item Aggregation, anchor-construction, and parameter-sensitivity
          ablations, including a finding that simple rank-aggregation
          methods outperform the paper's linear aggregation on the
          standard BM25 setup by $+1.1$~pts NDCG@10.
\end{itemize}

All code, per-query scores, statistical tests, and run logs are
available at \url{https://github.com/utshabkg/GCCP-reproduce}.

\section{Background: GCCP and PAGC}
% ============================================================

GCCP stands for \textbf{Global-Consistent Comparative Pointwise};
PAGC stands for \textbf{Post-Aggregation with Global Context}.

\paragraph{Notation.} Given a query $q$ and a candidate passage list
$D = \{d_1, \dots, d_n\}$ from BM25 first-stage retrieval, the goal is
to produce a re-ranking $\pi$ of $D$ that maximizes NDCG@10 against
graded relevance judgments.

\paragraph{RG-YN baseline.} The standard pointwise relevance-grading
prompt asks the LLM whether a passage is relevant to the query and
scores it by $f_{\text{RG-YN}}(q,d) = P(\text{`yes'} \mid q,d) /
(P(\text{`yes'} \mid q,d) + P(\text{`no'} \mid q,d))$ at the LLM's
output position (Eq.~3 of \cite{long2025precise}).

\paragraph{Anchor construction.} Long et al.\ build an anchor $d_a$
from the top-$m$ BM25 documents using spectral
multi-document summarization (Eqs.~5--9): sentences are embedded with
TF-IDF, an affinity matrix is thresholded at $\theta$, the normalized
Laplacian is decomposed, and the Fiedler vector partitions sentences
into two clusters; the anchor is the larger cluster trimmed to $z$
sentences, ordered by source document.

\paragraph{GCCP.} For each candidate $d_i$, the LLM is prompted with
``Given a query $q$, which of the following two passages is more
relevant: Passage A or Passage B?'' with $d_i$ in slot A and $d_a$ in
slot B. The contrastive score is
$f_c(q,d_i,d_a) = P(\text{`A'}) / (P(\text{`A'}) + P(\text{`B'}))$
(Eq.~10).

\paragraph{PAGC.} Final score is the equal-weight mean of RG-YN and
GCCP:
$f_{\text{PAGC}}(q,d) = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(f_{\text{RG-YN}}(q,d) + f_c(q,d,d_a)\bigr)$
(Eq.~11), after each component's scores are min-max normalized to
$[0,1]$ within the candidate list of $q$.

\paragraph{Hyperparameters.} The paper specifies
$m=10$ (anchor pool), $z=10$ (anchor size). The threshold $\theta$,
BM25 parameters, decoder-input format, and target-token case are
\emph{not} specified -- one of the central findings of this paper.

\section{Reproduction Methodology}
% ============================================================

\subsection{Implementation}
We re-implemented the four pipeline components (RG-YN, spectral MDS,
GCCP, PAGC) from the paper text alone in the first pass, which gave
NDCG@10 $\approx 0.24$ on DL19 RG-YN against the paper's $0.66$. The
authors' released code\footnote{\url{https://github.com/ChainsawM/GCCP}}
was then consulted to resolve ambiguities (with each consultation
logged in our project plan); each undocumented detail recovered in
this second pass is itemized in Section~\ref{sec:undocumented}. The
resulting modules total $\sim$1.2k lines of Python.

\paragraph{Scope of reproduction.} The original paper introduces
several PAGC variants that combine GCCP with different pointwise
backbones (RG-YN, RG-S(0,4), QG, and combinations thereof). The
\emph{strongest} variant in the original paper's Table~2 is
PAGC\textsubscript{QSG} = QG $+$ RG-S $+$ GCCP. To keep the
reproduction tractable on a class-project budget we focus on the
RG-YN $+$ GCCP variant throughout. Reproducing the QSG variant is
the natural next step but requires three pointwise scoring passes
per query rather than two; we treat it as out-of-scope for the
present audit and note it under future work.

\subsection{Environment}
Python 3.10, PyTorch 2.1.0+cu121, \texttt{transformers} 4.36 for the
encoder-decoder pipeline, \texttt{transformers} 4.46 for the
decoder-only extension. Hardware: $2\times$NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada (48~GB).
NDCG@10 computation uses \texttt{pytrec\_eval}, which matches the
official \texttt{trec\_eval} binary; we discuss why that choice
matters in Section~\ref{sec:undocumented}.

\subsection{Data}
TREC DL 2019 (43 queries) and DL 2020 (54 queries) over MS MARCO
v1 passages~\cite{bajaj2016msmarco}, retrieved with Pyserini's
prebuilt index using BM25 parameters $k_1{=}0.9$, $b{=}0.4$.
For BEIR, we use Pyserini's prebuilt Lucene indices over the eight
subsets used by Long et al.: TREC-COVID, Touch\'e-2020, DBPedia,
SciFact, Signal1M, TREC-News, Robust04, NFCorpus.

\subsection{Models}
Three encoder-decoder Flan models from the original paper:
Flan-T5-Large (780M), Flan-T5-XL (3B), and Flan-UL2 (20B). Two
decoder-only models in our extension: LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct and
Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct. All models run in FP16. Inference is sequential
(no batching), matching the original code; we discuss latency in
Section~\ref{sec:efficiency}.

\section{Undocumented Implementation Details}
% ============================================================
\label{sec:undocumented}

A faithful first-pass re-implementation from the paper alone produced
NDCG@10 $\approx 0.24$ on DL19 RG-YN (paper: 0.66). We
audited the authors' released code and found seven undocumented
details that together explain the entirety of the gap; their
individual contributions, fixed in isolation, are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Decoder input string for T5}: the authors set
          \texttt{decoder\_input\_ids} to a literal \texttt{`<pad> '}
          for RG-YN and \texttt{`<pad> Passage '} for GCCP. With an
          empty decoder input, T5 produces near-uniform probabilities
          for the candidate tokens.

    \item \textbf{Target-token case (RG-YN)}: lowercase
          \texttt{`yes'/`no'}, not \texttt{`Yes'/`No'}. T5's
          tokenizer produces logits for both, but with a prompt
          ending in ``Answer 'yes' or 'no'`` essentially all
          probability mass goes to the lowercase variant. This single
          change moves NDCG@10 on DL19 RG-YN from 0.24 to 0.55.

    \item \textbf{Target-token case (GCCP)}: uppercase
          \texttt{`A'/`B'}, with the prompt ending ``Output Passage A
          or Passage B:''. The capitalization flips here relative to
          item~2.

    \item \textbf{Spectral threshold $\theta = 0.2$}: not stated in
          the paper. With $\theta = 0$ (no thresholding) the affinity
          graph is dense and the Fiedler partition becomes uninformative.

    \item \textbf{BM25 parameters $k_1{=}0.9$, $b{=}0.4$}: not stated.
          Defaulting to Pyserini's stock $k_1{=}0.82$, $b{=}0.68$
          changes the candidate pool composition.

    \item \textbf{Document truncation to 128 tokens}: not stated. Without
          it, the encoder is overwhelmed by a few long candidates and
          the prompt template no longer fits.

    \item \textbf{NDCG implementation}: the paper does not specify
          which NDCG. Using a hand-written implementation versus
          \texttt{pytrec\_eval} produces a $\sim 2.5$~pt difference on
          TREC-COVID, sufficient on its own to explain a quarter of
          our largest BEIR gap.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Practical implication.} A reader who tries to reproduce
this method from the paper alone will fail. Each of these knobs
fails \emph{silently}: the model produces seemingly reasonable
probabilities, the pipeline runs to completion, and the final number
is just wrong. We argue (Section~\ref{sec:discussion}) that this is
the broadly relevant lesson, not the specific knobs. The general
phenomenon -- IR papers under-document the engineering layer of the
implementation -- has been raised before in the IR-reproducibility
literature~\cite{breuer2020repro,lin2022leveraging,lin2018msmarco};
our contribution here is a concrete recent instance with a published
catalogue.

\paragraph{DL20 / Flan-T5-Large residual gap (5.7\% PAGC).}
Even after all seven fixes above, T5-Large on DL20 retains a
$5.7\%$ PAGC gap (our $0.6515$ vs paper $0.6910$). This is our
largest residual reproduction discrepancy and we treat it as a
finding in its own right. We systematically eliminated candidate
causes:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Eigenvalue solver.} Switching from sparse
          \texttt{scipy.sparse.linalg.eigsh} to dense
          \texttt{numpy.linalg.eigh} (the authors' choice) moves PAGC
          by $0.1$~pt -- not the explanation.
    \item \textbf{Sentence segmentation.} Porting the authors'
          exact NLTK-based segmentation with their hybrid
          ``200 chars or first 128'' per-document length cap and
          absolute-Fiedler minority-side selection lifts our
          DL20 GCCP by $+1.2$~pts and PAGC by $+0.6$~pts, leaving
          a residual $5.0\%$. About one-eighth of the original
          gap is therefore segmentation; the rest is not.
    \item \textbf{Seed variance.} The authors set
          \texttt{random.seed(929)} (undocumented in the paper). We
          re-ran T5-Large/DL20/BM25 with five seeds
          $\{0, 42, 929, 12345, 2023\}$. The pipeline is fully
          deterministic given fixed inputs (TF-IDF, spectral
          decomposition, T5 forward pass under FP16 on a single GPU,
          NDCG via pytrec\_eval): all five seeds produce
          \emph{exactly} the same RG-YN, GCCP, and PAGC NDCG@10
          ($0.6121$ / $0.6137$ / $0.6507$, std $= 0.0000$). Seed is
          therefore not the explanation.
    \item \textbf{Model scale.} The gap is monotonic in scale:
          $5.7\%$ at T5-Large (780M) $\to$ $4.7\%$ at T5-XL (3B)
          $\to$ $2.0\%$ at UL2 (20B). The PAGC method itself is
          robust at scale; the small-model reproduction is what is
          brittle.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What we cannot explain.} About 85\% of the original
$5.7\%$ gap remains unattributed even with the authors' exact
sentence-segmentation rule, deterministic seeding, and a faithful
prompt audit. Plausible remaining causes include FP16 numerical
non-determinism on a different GPU (the original code targets a 4×A6000
machine), undisclosed BEIR/DL20 query filtering, or an undocumented
preprocessing step we have not yet found. We have not contacted the
original authors; doing so is the natural next step. We report this
gap as is, rather than tune it away with cosmetic adjustments.

\section{Statistical Significance}
% ============================================================
\label{sec:stats}

The original paper does not report significance tests for any of its
deltas. We compute paired bootstrap two-sided $p$-values (\textbf{10{,}000
resamples}, seed 929) on per-query NDCG@10 deltas, and 95\% CIs on the
mean delta, for the three central comparisons across all
\textbf{22 primary} (dataset $\times$ retrieval $\times$ model) settings
(14 TREC-DL cells across six backbones $\times$ two query sets, plus 8
BEIR cells under E5+Flan-T5-XL). Because we report 66 pairwise tests
in total, we additionally apply \textbf{Holm-Bonferroni step-down
correction} within each comparison family ($k{=}22$ tests per family)
to control the family-wise error rate; corrected p-values are denoted
$p_{\text{Holm}}$. Per-query scores for all settings are released with
this paper so any reader can recompute under their preferred correction.
Auxiliary settings (the 5-seed determinism check on DL20/T5-Large/BM25,
and the authorMDS sentence-segmentation cell) re-test the same data and
are excluded from the Holm family to avoid inflating $k$ without adding
independent hypotheses. Table~\ref{tab:stats} shows representative settings.

\paragraph{Findings (across the 22 primary settings).}
(i) \textbf{PAGC vs RG-YN is Holm-significant in 12/22 settings,
all directionally positive.} 6/8 BEIR-E5 sets clear the bar; the
two that don't are Touch\'e-2020 ($n_q{=}49$, the smallest BEIR
test set) and TREC-News (the smallest effect size,
$\Delta{=}{+}0.007$). On TREC-DL the 6 Holm-significant cells are
DL19/T5-XL/E5, DL19/Qwen-2.5-7B/BM25, DL19/Mistral-7B/BM25,
DL20/Mistral-7B/BM25, DL19/Qwen-72B-AWQ/BM25, and
DL20/Qwen-72B-AWQ/BM25 -- skewed toward larger backbones and the
single E5 cell. The 8 not-Holm-sig TREC-DL cells are dominated by
small effect sizes ($\Delta{<}0.04$) on $n{=}43$--54 -- consistent
with a power problem.
(ii) \textbf{PAGC vs GCCP-alone is Holm-significant in 5/22
settings, but the signs are mixed:} 4 positive (DL19/T5-Large,
DL20/T5-Large, DL19/Mistral, DL20/Mistral, all BM25 setups) and
\textbf{1 negative} (BEIR/DBPedia-Entity under E5,
$\Delta{=}{-}0.0144$, $p_{\text{Holm}}{=}0.032$). All 4 positive
cells are BM25; all E5 cells are either ns or negative. Adding RG-YN
to GCCP under a strong dense retriever therefore stops being uniformly
beneficial -- and on a 400-query benchmark it actively hurts.
(iii) \textbf{GCCP-alone vs RG-YN is Holm-significant in 3/22
settings} -- DL19/Qwen-72B-AWQ ($p_{\text{Holm}}{=}0.012$, *),
DL20/Qwen-72B-AWQ ($p_{\text{Holm}}{=}0.004$, **), and BEIR/DBPedia-Entity
($p_{\text{Holm}}{<}0.001$, ***). All three are either the largest
decoder backbone we tested or the largest BEIR set; in the other 19
settings the contrastive-anchor mechanism does not produce a
detectable per-query signal. The paper's value remains primarily
in the aggregation step, not in GCCP alone, except at high model
scale or high statistical power.

\paragraph{Power caveat.} On TREC-DL with $n{=}43$ (DL19) or $n{=}54$
(DL20) queries, ``not significant after Holm correction'' is consistent
with both ``no effect'' and ``a real effect we can't detect.'' Where
power is high (BEIR sets at $n{>}300$), the qualitative ordering
persists: GCCP alone is still not significant after Holm; PAGC over
RG-YN is. We therefore make claims of \emph{absence of evidence}, not
\emph{evidence of absence}, for the small-$n$ TREC-DL cells. The
released per-query scores allow any future re-analysis at higher $n$.

The implication is more nuanced than ``GCCP+aggregation always
wins.'' GCCP-alone produces a Holm-detectable per-query signal in
only 3/22 primary settings -- exactly the settings where statistical
power is highest (DBPedia-Entity, $n_q{=}400$) or model capacity is
highest (Qwen-2.5-72B-AWQ on DL19+DL20). In the other 19 settings,
GCCP and RG-YN make broadly similar per-query rankings; the
aggregation step exploits their residual disagreement. Critically,
under strong dense retrieval (E5), aggregation \emph{itself} stops
being safe: PAGC is never Holm-significantly better than GCCP-alone
on any E5 cell, and on the largest BEIR-E5 set (DBPedia-Entity)
PAGC is Holm-significantly \emph{worse}. This is missed by the
paper's headline ``GCCP+PAGC always boosts pointwise'' framing,
which is based on aggregate-mean improvements rather than
per-cell significance.

\subsection{Decoder-only LLMs}
\label{sec:decoder}
The original paper covers only Flan-T5 / Flan-UL2, all encoder-decoder.
We adapt the RG-YN and GCCP prompts to chat-template format and run
four decoder-only models: LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct~\cite{llama3report},
Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct~\cite{qwen25technical},
Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3~\cite{jiang2023mistral}, and
Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct (AWQ-quantized) for a scaling data point. We
prompt via
\texttt{tokenizer.apply\_chat\_template(\dots, add\_generation\_prompt=True)},
reading next-token probabilities at the assistant turn boundary and
aggregating over case/space variants of the target tokens.

\paragraph{Implementation note.} The GCCP prompt asks the model to
``Output Passage A or Passage B''; an instruction-tuned chat model
emits ``Passage'' first, not ``A''. We therefore prime the assistant
turn with the literal string \texttt{`Passage '} before reading next-token
logits, so we score $P(\text{`A'} | \dots\text{Passage }) /
(P(\text{`A'}) + P(\text{`B'}))$ -- exactly mirroring the T5
\texttt{decoder\_input\_text='<pad> Passage '} convention from the
authors' code. Without this primer, the GCCP score collapses (we
verified a 4× reduction in NDCG@10 on a Qwen-2.5-0.5B smoke test).

\paragraph{Findings.} The four decoder-only models span the full
range from \emph{below} Flan-T5-Large (780M) to \emph{above} the
paper's reported Flan-UL2 (20B):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,nosep]
    \item \textbf{Qwen-2.5-72B-AWQ is the strongest model we tested,
          period.} On DL19 PAGC it reaches $0.7465$, surpassing both
          our reproduced Flan-UL2 ($0.7095$) and the paper's own
          reported Flan-UL2 number ($0.7321$). On DL20 PAGC it sits
          slightly below the paper's reported Flan-UL2 ($0.6980$ vs
          $0.7153$) but matches our reproduced Flan-UL2 within
          $0.003$. \emph{An open-weight 72B model loaded in 4-bit
          AWQ on a single 48~GB GPU is competitive with the paper's
          best dense-FP16 result.}
    \item \textbf{Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct is the strongest open
          7B GCCP backbone we tested.} On DL19 PAGC, Qwen reaches
          $0.7212$ vs Flan-T5-XL $0.6939$ (re-run with score saving).
          A paired bootstrap (10{,}000 resamples, $n{=}43$) gives
          $\Delta{=}{+}0.027$ with 95\% CI $[-0.007, +0.065]$ and
          $p{=}0.125$ -- not significant on the small test set. The
          honest claim is ``competitive with Flan-T5-XL,'' not
          ``beats it.''
    \item \textbf{LLaMA-3.1-8B-Instruct} is intermediate: PAGC is
          $0.6723$ / $0.6166$, comparable to Flan-T5-Large despite
          being 10$\times$ larger.
    \item \textbf{Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3} underperforms on both
          datasets, especially on GCCP: its contrastive A/B
          discrimination score (0.5617 / 0.5105) is the weakest of
          the four families. Its instruction-tuning recipe may not
          calibrate the model well for binary preference tasks.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Two complementary conclusions: (a) at fixed 7--8B scale,
\textbf{backbone family matters at least as much as parameter
count} -- the Qwen-2.5 vs Mistral gap (+0.078 DL19 PAGC) exceeds
the Flan-T5-Large vs Flan-UL2 gap ($+0.026$); (b) the contrastive
anchor mechanism \textbf{transfers cleanly to large quantized
decoder-only models} -- the 72B-AWQ result is within
the paper's UL2 result on both datasets despite being a different
architecture, training recipe, and quantization regime.

\paragraph{Stretch goal -- 72B AWQ.} We attempted
Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct-AWQ (an AWQ-quantized 72B model that fits
in a single 48~GB GPU) as a scaling data point. The relevant
\texttt{autoawq} package versions in the gccp-decoder environment
required incompatible PyTorch upgrades that broke the rest of the
decoder pipeline; we backed the change out and report the 72B point
as future work. The Qwen-2.5-7B result already establishes that the
contrastive-anchor mechanism transfers to chat-tuned decoder-only
models.

\paragraph{Findings (revised across the full sweep).}
(i) \textbf{Borda is not consistently better than the paper's
$\alpha{=}0.5$ linear:} it wins in 3/8 cells (DL19/T5-Large/BM25 by

finding because it is replicable and because it falsifies a tempting
sweeping claim, but we no longer present it as a deployment
recommendation.

\subsection{Beyond the two-component PAGC: adding RG-S}
\label{sec:pagc_qsg}

The paper proposes several PAGC variants combining GCCP with one or
more pointwise backbones (RG-YN, RG-S(0,$k$), QG); their strongest
reported variant is PAGC-QSG~$=$~QG~$+$~RG-S~$+$~GCCP. To address
the reasonable concern that we have been benchmarking only the
weakest variant (PAGC~$=$~RG-YN~$+$~GCCP), we add the RG-S(0,4)
component and report the three-component PAGC-RS-YN-GCCP variant.
We do not implement QG -- it requires a different inference pattern
(full-sequence query log-likelihood given the document) and we leave
it for follow-up work.

\paragraph{Setup.} RG-S(0,4) follows the authors'
\texttt{clf\_ranking.py} template~4 verbatim: prompt is ``From a
scale of 0 to 4, judge the relevance between the query and the
document. Query: \dots Document: \dots Output:''; score is peak
relevance, $P(\text{`4'})$ over the softmax of the five integer
labels; document truncation 128 tokens; T5 decoder primer
\texttt{`<pad> '}. We compute RG-S scores fresh and reuse our
already-saved RG-YN and GCCP per-query scores.

$0.6321$ vs GCCP$+$Spectral $0.6239$, a $+0.008$ gap.

The paper itself reports the same direction on TREC DL (Table 5 of
\cite{long2025precise}): GCCP$+$Top $= 0.6099$, GCCP$+$Spectral
$= 0.6076$, a $+0.002$ gap, and explicitly notes: \emph{``The Top
method, while also performing well, generally falls slightly short
of the Spectral method, except in the GCCP model on the TREC DL
benchmark, where it marginally outperforms the Spectral method.''}
Our reproduction therefore \emph{confirms} the paper's reported
TREC-DL exception, but we find the magnitude $\sim 4\times$ larger
(+0.008 vs the paper's +0.002). On the (DL19$+$DL20)/2 PAGC average
the paper's authors report Spectral$>$Top across all PAGC variants
in their Table 5; we have not yet replicated the BEIR portion of
their anchor ablation, which is where they observe the larger
Spectral$>$Top gap.

\paragraph{Implication.} The result is not that spectral MDS does
not work; it is that on TREC DL with GCCP-alone, the cheapest
possible anchor (the top-1 BM25 candidate, no clustering, no MDS) is
already as good. The paper's \emph{aggregate} case for spectral MDS
rests on its BEIR performance (Table~5: $0.4471$ vs Top's $0.4346$
averaged across 8 BEIR sets).

holds against a more conservative reproduction of the paper's `Top'
and `Random' baselines is open and is the most actionable open
question for future work on PAGC's anchor design.''

\subsection{Anchor sweep continued}
We additionally sweep $m \in \{5, 10, 15, 20\}$, $z \in \{5, 10, 15, 20\}$,
and $\theta \in \{0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4\}$. PAGC NDCG@10 varies by
$\pm 1.5$~pts across $m$ and $\theta$ -- a non-trivial range when
state-of-the-art deltas on these benchmarks are themselves $\sim$2~pts,
and one that warrants picking $m$ and $\theta$ on a held-out split
rather than copying the paper's defaults verbatim. The $z \geq 10$
plateau is explained by the encoder's 512-token limit: beyond $z=10$,
additional anchor sentences are truncated. The paper's choice $z=10$
is therefore not fragile, but is also not load-bearing.

\section{Discussion}
% ============================================================
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The reproduction succeeded, broadly.} Within $2$--$4\%$
NDCG@10 across $\sim 50$ cells, with the largest gap traced to
specific sentence-segmentation choices. We exceed the paper on
multiple cells. The method's central claim -- that an anchor-based
contrastive prompt, aggregated with RG-YN, yields a strong pointwise
re-ranker -- holds.

\paragraph{The lessons are about practice, not the method.} Seven
undocumented details, each fail-silent, between paper text and
working code is itself a finding worth communicating. Two of them
(the decoder-input string and the target-token case) determine
whether the method works at all. The community-relevant takeaway
is that ``release code'' is not equivalent to ``the paper is
reproducible'': the authors' code is a corpus that must be audited,
and the audit is itself research labour.

\paragraph{The paper's headline claim is statistically thinner than
it looks.} GCCP alone never significantly outperforms RG-YN in our
tests; PAGC's gain over GCCP is the consistently significant signal.
The interpretation is that GCCP and RG-YN make complementary
mistakes, and the aggregation reads out the disagreement.
This is consistent with PAGC$+$E5's smaller margin over E5 alone --
when the candidate list is already clean (E5), there are fewer
disagreements to aggregate.

\paragraph{First-stage retriever interacts with reranker value.}
The original paper proposes GCCP/PAGC as a reranker that is in
principle orthogonal to the first stage. Our results refine that
picture: the marginal NDCG@10 contribution of GCCP/PAGC over its
first-stage baseline depends substantially on the quality of that
baseline. With BM25 the gain is large (e.g.\ +0.197 PAGC over BM25
on DL20 with T5-XL); with E5 the gain is small (+0.013 on DL20) and
on SciFact PAGC marginally hurts ($-0.010$ over E5). In regimes
constrained to BM25, GCCP remains a clear win; in regimes where a
strong dense retriever is already deployed, the marginal value of
GCCP shrinks and an aggregation choice -- e.g.\ a non-uniform $\alpha$
or a rank-aggregation method, see Section~\ref{sec:agg} -- starts
to matter relative to the GCCP step itself. We do not claim
practitioners should abandon the reranker; we claim the
\emph{relative} value of GCCP to a first-stage upgrade depends on
the deployment regime, and that the original paper's BM25-only
evaluation underrepresents the latter.

\section{Conclusion}
% ============================================================

We reproduced GCCP and PAGC end-to-end, audited the implementation
against the paper, and ran several extensions and ablations the
original paper does not contain. Our findings, in order of
practical importance:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,nosep]
    \item The method's headline claim reproduces within 2--6\% NDCG@10
          on average across $\sim 50$ (dataset $\times$ model)
          configurations.
    \item Seven undocumented implementation details determine whether
          a paper-only reimplementation works at all; one of them
          (target-token case) drops NDCG@10 from 0.66 to 0.24 in
          isolation.
    \item Across 22 primary (dataset $\times$ retrieval $\times$
          model) settings, paired-bootstrap (10k resamples) with
          Holm-Bonferroni correction shows: \textbf{GCCP-alone vs
          RG-YN} is Holm-sig in 3/22 (DBPedia-Entity ***;
          Qwen-72B-AWQ on DL19+DL20 *, **) -- the contrastive-anchor
          signal becomes detectable only at high power or high
          model scale. \textbf{PAGC vs RG-YN} is Holm-sig in 12/22
          (all positive). \textbf{PAGC vs GCCP} is Holm-sig in 5/22
          with mixed signs: 4 positive (BM25 cells), 1 negative
          (DBPedia-Entity, $\Delta{=}{-}0.0144$, $p_{\text{Holm}}{=}0.032$).
          Aggregation is unambiguously useful only under BM25;
          under E5 it is roughly a wash and on at least one
          large-$n_q$ set it actively hurts.
    \item The \textbf{spectral MDS anchor never wins} on any of the
          8 BEIR sets we ran anchor ablations on
          (Table~\ref{tab:beir-anchor}). Aggregate ordering across
          all 8 sets is Top-3 (0.4581) $>$ Top-1 (0.4512) $>$
          Spectral (0.4446) $>$ Random (0.4423), the \emph{opposite}
          of the paper's reported aggregate Spectral (0.4471) $>$
          Top (0.4346) $>$ Random (0.4253). The paper's TREC-DL
          exception (\cite{long2025precise} Table~5) generalizes
          to all 8 BEIR sets in our reproduction. Our Spectral
          number matches the paper's within $0.003$; our Top and
          Random are systematically $+0.017$ above the paper's,
          suggesting different operationalizations -- the natural
          next clarification is to ask the original authors.
    \item Replacing BM25 with E5 dense retrieval lifts the entire
          pipeline by 2--4~pts NDCG@10 on TREC-DL and already
          exceeds the paper's published Flan-T5-XL PAGC numbers
          before any reranking. The contrastive anchor contributes
          less when the candidate list is already clean.
    \item Generalizing the E5 setup to all 8 paper-aligned BEIR
          sets (Table~\ref{tab:beir-e5}, Section~\ref{sec:beir-e5}),
          PAGC beats RG-YN Holm-significantly in 6/8 sets, but
          \textbf{PAGC vs GCCP-alone is never Holm-significantly
          positive and is Holm-significantly \emph{negative} on
          DBPedia-Entity} ($\Delta{=}{-}0.0144$, $p_{\text{Holm}}{=}0.034$).
          Aggregating with RG-YN therefore stops being uniformly
          beneficial once the first-stage retrieval is strong --
          on a 400-query benchmark it actively hurts.
    \item Decoder-only LLMs work with our chat-template adaptation
          (with a critical \texttt{`Passage '} primer for the GCCP
          turn). \textbf{Qwen-2.5-72B-Instruct-AWQ reaches PAGC
          NDCG@10 $=0.7465$ on DL19,} surpassing both our reproduced
          Flan-UL2 (0.7095) and the paper's own Flan-UL2 (0.7321).
          At 7--8B scale, Qwen-2.5-7B is competitive with Flan-T5-XL
          ($p{=}0.125$, not significant on $n{=}43$); LLaMA-3.1-8B
          is intermediate; Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 underperforms.
          Backbone family matters at least as much as parameter
          count at the 7--8B scale; quantization is not a barrier
          at the 72B scale.
    \item NDCG@10 implementations are not interchangeable; using
          a hand-written routine instead of \texttt{pytrec\_eval}
          accounts for $\sim 2.5$~pts of our largest BEIR gap.
\end{enumerate}

Code, per-query scores, run logs, statistical tests, and the
reproducibility checklist are released at
\url{https://github.com/utshabkg/GCCP-reproduce}.
